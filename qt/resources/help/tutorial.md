# Tutorial: Xournal Qt in 15 minutes

This document is yours to write on. Every page asks you to try one thing; the space below the text is for it. Go
through it in order, or jump to what you need: the **Contents** in the sidebar lists the sections (the slim tab at the
left edge of the page opens the sidebar).

This is a **copy** for you. It is kept in the app's own folder, not in your library, so nothing here clutters your
folders. To keep it with your documents, choose **⋮ → Save as…** and a folder of your library. Help → **Start the
tutorial again** gives you a fresh copy.

> right, the page, the pill with the page number at the bottom right, the slim sidebar tab at the left edge.

This tutorial is a **text document inside a PDF**: the text is Markdown that you can still edit with the keyboard, and
the pen writes on top of it. You will learn more about that on the Markdown pages.

## 1. Write with the pen

Your tools lie in the **toolbox** beside the page (at the right; drag its dotted grip to another edge). Take a
**pen** there (a tap, or the key **P**) and write your name in the space below.

- With a pen on a touch screen, the pen writes and your **finger scrolls and zooms** (pinch). Your hand can rest on
  the screen while you write.
- On a phone, the toolbox is the dock at the bottom; **My tools** at its end has all of them.
- Without a pen, Settings → Touch → "Draw with the finger" (or the finger button in the toolbox) lets the finger
  write.

## 2. Color and width

Each tool of the toolbox has its own **color** and **width**: the toolbox starts with three pens (body text, key
terms, warnings) and two highlighters.

- Take another pen, then write a word in it.
- Tap the pen in your hand again: its **editor** opens beside it, with the colors of the palette, the colors used
  lately, the width (a slider, and five sizes) and the line style. A change counts at once.
- The mouse wheel over a tool changes its width; **+** at the end of the toolbox adds a tool of your own.

Try three colors and two widths below.

## 3. Highlight

Take a **highlighter** from the toolbox (or press **H**) and highlight the sentence below.

**Highlight me: the highlighter is translucent, so the text stays readable.**

On a PDF with text (a paper, lecture slides) there is more: **Mark PDF text** (the tool with the marked line of text)
highlights, underlines or strikes through the PDF's own text when you drag over it. Tap that tool again, or press and
hold it, to choose how it marks. Try it on one of your PDFs later.

## 4. Erase

Write a few words below, then take the **eraser** (or press **E**) and erase one of them.

The eraser has three kinds; tap it again while it is in hand to choose in its editor:

- **Eraser**: removes what it touches.
- **Whiteout**: paints white over it.
- **Whole strokes**: removes every stroke it touches, completely.

Its size is in its editor too. With many pens, the button on the pen erases while you hold it.

## 5. Select and move

Draw a small house below. Then take **Select** (the dashed rectangle, or **S**; tap it again for the lasso, or press
**L**) and draw a frame around the house.

- Drag the selection to move it.
- Drag a corner to make it bigger or smaller; turn it with its rotation handle.
- **Delete** removes it; **Ctrl+C** and **Ctrl+V** copy and paste it, also into another document.
- A tap outside ends the selection.

## 6. Undo and redo

Draw a line below, then undo it.

- **Ctrl+Z** undoes, **Ctrl+Y** (or Ctrl+Shift+Z) redoes.
- The arrows are also at the start of the toolbox (on a phone: in the dock).
- On a touch screen: a **tap with two fingers** undoes, a tap with three fingers redoes.

Everything you do with pages (adding, moving, deleting them) can be undone the same way.

## 7. Pages

Go to the **last page** of this tutorial and add a page after it: **Add a page** in the tool bar (or **Ctrl+N**). Press
and hold the button to choose the background (lined, squared, plain …), the size and how many pages.

- **All pages** (the grid button in the pill at the bottom right, or **Ctrl+Alt+G**) shows every page at once. Pinch,
  or use − and +, to change the number of columns. Tap a page to go there.
- The **sidebar** (the slim tab at the left edge) shows the pages, the layers, the contents and the annotations.
- Press and hold a page in the sidebar or the grid, or tap its ⋮: insert, duplicate, move, delete, bookmark it.
  Several pages can be selected and dragged to another place.

## 8. Search

Press **Ctrl+F** (or the magnifier in the tool bar) and search for **turbine**, a word on this page. The hits are
marked on the pages; Enter goes to the next one, Shift+Enter back.

Now search for **turbnie**: nothing is found. Turn on **Fuzzy** in the search bar and search again: the fuzzy search
finds words with a letter left out, added, swapped or wrong. Press and hold the Fuzzy button (or right-click it) for
its help: `a b` finds both words, `a | b` either of them, `!a` documents without it.

Search goes further than this document:

- **All open documents**: in the overview of all documents (next section), or **Ctrl+Shift+F**.
- **The whole library**: the search field of the library, or **Ctrl+Alt+F**. It finds the text of PDFs too, also of
  ones you have not opened in months, and shows the hits on the pages.

## 9. Tabs and the overview

Every document opens in a **tab**. Open a second one: the **+** at the end of the tab strip makes a new document.

- **Ctrl+Tab** goes to the next tab, Ctrl+Shift+Tab back.
- **All open documents** (the grid button in the tab strip, or **Ctrl+Shift+E**) shows every tab as a card with its
  current page. "Search all documents" there searches them all.
- On a phone there is no tab strip: the number in the top bar counts your tabs (tap it for the overview); a swipe
  along the top bar switches to the next document.

## 10. A document beside another (the reference)

Writing notes while reading a paper? Put the paper **beside** your notes.

- Right-click (or press and hold) the tab of another document and choose **Open as reference**. It shows beside the
  current one (above it on an upright screen), read-only, with a pill of its own: pages, copy, swap sides, close.
- **Show this document beside** in the menu of the current tab shows the same document twice: two places of one
  document, for example the exercises beside the lecture.

Try it: open this tutorial beside itself and scroll the two sides independently.

## 11. Full screen and presenting

- **Full screen** (the button in the tool bar, or **F11**): only the page, the toolbox floating at its edge (its ⋯:
  present, read only, search, settings, leave full screen) and the page pill. Esc leaves it.
- **Present** (**F5**): full screen on black, one page at a time, from the current page; the arrow keys, Page Up and
  Page Down or a swipe go on. Esc goes back to full-screen editing.
- **Present without controls** (Ctrl+F5, or press and hold the Present button): only the page.
- **Read only** (**⋮ → View → Read**, or **Ctrl+Alt+R**; in full screen also the toolbox's ⋯): full screen where the
  pen and the fingers do not write. Tap the left or the right edge of the page for the previous or the next page; a
  swipe still scrolls, and text of a PDF can still be selected. The lock in the corner (or Ctrl+Alt+R) lets you write
  again, Esc leaves full screen.

## 12. Markdown on a page

Text you type can be **Markdown**, shown formatted while you type: headings with `#`, **bold** with `**`, lists with
`-`, check lists with `- [ ]`, formulas with `$…$` (like $E = mc^2$), tables, code and pictures.

- On any page of notes: **Write on the page** in the toolbox (or **Ctrl+Alt+M**), then type.
- **Text box** (**T**): tap somewhere on the page and type a Markdown text box there.
- The **formatting bar** under the tool bar does it for you if you do not know the syntax.

This tutorial is Markdown too: take the text box tool (**T**), tap into this paragraph and change a word; Esc ends
writing. (Typing without a tap goes on at the end of the text on the page in view.) Handwriting stays where you drew
it, also when the text above it gets longer: move it yourself with Select if it should go along.

## 13. Markdown documents

A Markdown text can also be a document of its own, in two ways:

- **A Markdown file** (`.md`): plain text that other apps (Obsidian, Typora, VS Code, GitHub) open too. Xournal Qt
  shows it formatted and edits it; nothing else is written into it.
- **A PDF text document**, like this tutorial: the text lives inside a PDF with notes. You write on it with the pen and
  keep editing the text with the keyboard; any PDF app shows it as a normal PDF, and the Markdown travels inside it
  (⋮ → Export → Export as Markdown gets it out again).

In the library, **New → New text document…** makes one; which of the two it is, Settings → Documents → "New text
documents" decides (when it is set to Markdown files, the entry reads "New Markdown file…"). A `.md` becomes a PDF text document with **⋮ → Document → Open as PDF document**; the `.md`
stays as it is.

## 14. Saving

**Ctrl+S** saves (or the Save button). How documents are kept, you chose at the first start (Settings → Documents
changes it):

- **PDF files**: every document is one PDF. Notes on a PDF go into that PDF itself, as annotations every PDF viewer
  shows, and the full notes stay editable inside it. Nothing else is written next to your files.
- **Xournal++ files**: notes are `.xopp` files next to their PDFs, which Xournal++ opens too.

More in the ⋮ menu: **Save as…** (a PDF with notes or a .xopp), **Share…**, **Print…**, and Export (a plain PDF, a
PDF/A for the archive, Markdown).

## 15. Versions

A PDF with notes can keep its **versions** inside itself: one for each day you save it, plus **milestones** you name.
They travel with the file; other PDF apps still show it as it is now. It is off until you turn it on.

- Open the sidebar's **History** (the clock after the highlighter button at the top of the sidebar; also ⋮ → Document
  → Version history…) and turn on **Keep versions of this document**. This tutorial is a PDF with notes: try it here.
- Write something and press **Ctrl+Alt+S** ("Save with a message…"): type what you did, e.g. "My first milestone".
  It is listed with a flag. Ctrl+S on the same day keeps one version for the day.
- Tap a version in the list: **Show beside the document**, **Restore this version…** (Undo brings today's back) or
  **Open as a copy**.
- Settings → Documents → **Keep versions of new PDFs with notes** turns it on for every new one. Share sends a PDF
  without its versions unless you tick "With its version history".

## 16. The library

The **library** is a folder; its subfolders and documents are what you see on the home screen (the button with the
library's name at the left end of the tab strip, or **Ctrl+Shift+L**).

- **Any folder can be a library**: the menu under the library's name has "Open a folder as library…". Nothing is
  imported; your documents stay where they are, and any sync app can keep them.
- **New** makes documents, text documents and folders; **Import** copies files from elsewhere into the library.
- Tap the **star** of a card to make it a favourite; the star at the top shows only your favourites.
- **Recent** lists what you opened lately, **Bookmarks** the pages you bookmarked (⋮ → Bookmark this page).
- **Show** chooses which kinds of files appear: notes, PDFs, Markdown, pictures, text, other files (in a narrow
  window it is under View).
